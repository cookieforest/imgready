The case for TIFF → WebP

If you have TIFFs and you need them on the web, you have two options: convert to JPG (the safe legacy choice) or convert to WebP (the modern default). For modern websites — anything published after about 2021 — WebP is almost always better. WebP at quality 82 is visually identical to a JPG at quality 90 but produces a file 25–35% smaller, and crucially WebP supports a full alpha channel, so TIFFs that contain transparency translate cleanly.

For archival web copies where pixel perfection matters (a photographer's portfolio of large-format work, a museum's digital reproduction), WebP-lossless is the closest thing to "TIFF on the web". Quality 100 produces lossless WebP, typically 26% smaller than the equivalent PNG with identical fidelity.

When to use lossy vs lossless WebP

  • Photography portfolio, e-commerce, blog hero, marketing image. Lossy WebP at quality 82. Visually identical, dramatically smaller.
  • Archive of museum reproductions, fine-art prints, scientific imaging. Lossless WebP (quality 100). Same fidelity as PNG/TIFF, smaller bytes.
  • Documents with text (scanned contracts, invoices). Lossless WebP — JPG and lossy WebP both make text fuzzier, lossless preserves every pixel.

Resize-during-convert is essential

TIFFs are typically very high resolution. A 24-megapixel TIFF rendered at full size on a 1200-pixel-wide blog hero is 70+ MB of unused pixels. Always set a resize target before converting. Sensible targets:

  • Hero image / large product photo: 1600 px on the long side.
  • Inline blog image: 1200 px.
  • Thumbnail / list item: 600 px.
  • Print-quality web archive: 3200 px (2x retina); store original TIFF separately.

Privacy

Like the TIFF → JPG path, TIFF → WebP runs entirely in your browser. Sensitive scans (tax records, medical images, business documents) never reach a server. This is a meaningful difference from cloud-based converters.

TIFF to WebP vs TIFF to AVIF

If you're weighing WebP against AVIF for your TIFF conversion, here's the practical breakdown: WebP produces smaller files than TIFF with ~97% browser support and very fast encoding. AVIF goes further — typically 20–30% smaller than WebP — but encodes slower and has ~93% browser support. For a static website doing a one-time batch conversion, AVIF is the better end destination. For a workflow where speed matters or where you need maximum platform support, WebP is the practical choice. See the AVIF vs WebP comparison for a full breakdown.

Frequently asked questions

Should I convert TIFF to WebP or JPG?

For modern websites, WebP. For email attachments, photo printing, and any older system that may not decode WebP, JPG is safer.

Will WebP preserve the alpha channel from a TIFF?

Yes. WebP has a full alpha channel and imgready preserves transparency through both lossy and lossless conversion.

How much smaller is the WebP than the source TIFF?

Usually 90%+ smaller. A typical 80 MB TIFF resized to 1600 pixels and saved as quality-82 WebP is around 200 KB.

Can I get pixel-perfect quality?

Yes — set the quality slider to 100 for lossless WebP. The result is identical to the resized TIFF in pixel terms.

Is there a TIFF compression imgready can't decode?

imgready decodes the three most common TIFF compressions: uncompressed, LZW, and PackBits. Less common compressions (Deflate, JPEG-in-TIFF, CCITT) may not decode — re-save as a more common compression in a desktop tool first.

Is TIFF to WebP private?

Yes. Everything is decoded and re-encoded in your browser. The file is never uploaded.
