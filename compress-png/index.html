Why PNG files are so often huge

PNG is a lossless raster format — every pixel is preserved exactly, including the transparency channel. That makes PNG perfect for screenshots, logos, icons, line art, UI mockups, and anything with sharp edges or transparency. The downside: a typical PNG export from Photoshop, Figma, or a screenshot tool can be several megabytes — much larger than the equivalent photograph saved as a JPG, despite often containing far less visual information. Optimizing PNG matters because PNGs end up everywhere on a website (logos, hero illustrations, UI screenshots) and the savings compound.

The two roads to a smaller PNG

Lossless optimization. The PNG file format itself supports several encoding strategies (filter selection, deflate level, color type). A "lazily-saved" PNG from Photoshop is often 20–40% larger than a fully optimized one. Tools like OxiPNG, OptiPNG, and pngcrush re-encode the file using the optimal settings without changing a single pixel — savings come purely from smarter compression of the same data.

Color quantization (PNG-8). Most "PNG files" people deal with are PNG-24 — full-color (16.7 million colors). Many real-world PNGs only need a fraction of that range — a flat illustration, an icon, a UI screenshot. Quantizing the palette down to 256 or fewer colors produces a PNG-8 file that is 40–70% smaller while typically being indistinguishable from the original at normal viewing. imgready uses UPNG.js (the same library Squoosh uses) to perform high-quality quantization in your browser.

How imgready compresses PNG

When the quality slider is below 100, imgready treats the request as "lossy PNG" and runs UPNG.js color quantization. UPNG analyses your image, builds an optimal palette of N colors (where N depends on the slider — 256 colors at quality 85+, dropping to 64 at quality 55, and 16 at quality 30), and re-encodes the image as a PNG-8 with that palette. The result is bit-for-bit a valid PNG that any browser, OS, or design tool will open identically; it just happens to be much smaller.

When the quality slider is at 100, imgready uses the browser's native canvas encoder for a fully lossless PNG. Both pipelines preserve the alpha channel, so transparent areas stay transparent. If you want even smaller files at the same fidelity, toggle to WebP — WebP-lossless is typically 26% smaller than PNG-lossless and is supported in every browser shipped since 2020.

A note on screenshots

Screenshots are the most common PNG most people produce — and they're also where PNG compression has the biggest impact. A typical 1080p screenshot with a few UI elements compresses to about 200 KB as PNG-8 (with imperceptible color shifts) or about 150 KB as WebP-lossless. The same screenshot at quality 100 PNG would be 600 KB or more. If you publish a lot of screenshots (documentation, blog posts, Stack Overflow answers), running them through imgready makes a noticeable difference to the size of your site or repository.

Frequently asked questions

Will PNG compression hurt the transparency?

No. imgready preserves the alpha channel through both PNG-8 quantization and WebP-lossless conversion. Transparent areas remain transparent in the output.

What is PNG-8 and is it really smaller than PNG-24?

PNG-8 stores at most 256 colors (or 8-bit indexed) instead of 16.7 million. For images with limited color variety — illustrations, icons, screenshots — PNG-8 is typically 40–70% smaller than PNG-24 with imperceptible visual difference.

How is this different from TinyPNG?

TinyPNG uploads your file to their server. imgready does the same kind of quantization (using UPNG.js) entirely in your browser — no upload, no quota, no signup, free.

Should I just convert PNG to WebP instead?

If your audience uses modern browsers (essentially everyone since 2020), WebP-lossless is typically 26% smaller than PNG-lossless with identical fidelity and full alpha support. Many sites benefit from doing both — keeping a PNG fallback for old browsers via the element.

Does compress-PNG work on transparent UI screenshots?

Yes — UI screenshots are exactly the kind of content PNG quantization handles best. Expect savings around 50–70%.

Why is my PNG still huge after compression?

Most likely it's a photograph saved as PNG. PNG is a lossless format and photographs simply do not compress losslessly into a small file. Convert to JPG (around 85 quality) or WebP — both will be 80%+ smaller.
