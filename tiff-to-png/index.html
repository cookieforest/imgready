Why convert TIFF to PNG instead of JPG?

The choice between PNG and JPG depends on your end goal. TIFF is always lossless and often 16-bit, making it perfect for archival masters. When you convert that master to a more portable format, PNG preserves every pixel while JPG compresses the image (losing data). Here's when to choose PNG over JPG:

  • Transparency matters: Your TIFF has an alpha channel (cutout, mask, or transparent background). JPG cannot store transparency; PNG can. This is the most common reason.
  • Editing master: You plan to edit the converted image further. Lossless PNG is safer than lossy JPG because no quality is lost during the first conversion—edits won't compound generation loss.
  • Archival in a compatible container: TIFF is the archival ideal, but PNG is widely understood by all modern software. If you need TIFF-like losslessness but better tool support, PNG is the middle ground.
  • Print pre-press: Some print workflows accept PNG as well as TIFF, and PNG's lossless nature makes it safer than JPG for downstream use.

If file size is your priority, JPG or WebP are smaller. If you need the smallest lossless output, WebP often beats PNG by 25–35%.

Handling alpha channels in TIFF

Some TIFF files, especially those created by professional design or photo software, include an alpha channel (transparency/mask). A common example is a product photo on a transparent background, exported from a design tool as a TIFF with cutout. When you convert to JPG, that transparency is lost and replaced with a solid background (usually white). PNG preserves the alpha channel exactly—what was transparent in the TIFF remains transparent in the PNG.

imgready detects alpha channels in TIFF automatically and writes them to PNG. Even 16-bit alpha is downsampled correctly to PNG's 8-bit alpha, so subtle masks are preserved as closely as the format allows.

File size expectations

PNG from TIFF is still large because both are lossless. A 24-megapixel TIFF and its PNG conversion are typically the same size—50 to 150 MB depending on color depth and compression. This is actually good news: it means imgready is not re-encoding or degrading the image.

If file size is your concern, consider:

  • JPG: Lossy compression shrinks the same image to 1–4 MB. Invisible quality loss at default settings.
  • WebP (lossless): About 25–35% smaller than PNG while remaining fully lossless. Good middle ground.
  • Resize first: Many TIFF masters are larger than needed for web or email. Resize during conversion to reduce byte count dramatically.

Frequently asked questions

Does TIFF to PNG lose quality?

No. Both TIFF and PNG are lossless formats, so conversion is a pixel-perfect copy. Every color value, bit of transparency, and detail from the original TIFF is preserved in the PNG output.

Why is the PNG still large?

PNG is lossless, so file size is similar to TIFF for photographic content. A 24-megapixel TIFF and its PNG conversion are typically the same size. For smaller files, use JPG (lossy, 10–50x reduction) or WebP (lossless or lossy, 25–35% smaller than PNG).

Does imgready preserve TIFF transparency?

Yes. TIFFs with alpha channels (transparency) are preserved in the PNG output. PNG has native support for 8-bit or 16-bit alpha channels, so even complex mask or cutout TIFFs convert cleanly.

What TIFF types does imgready support?

Uncompressed, LZW, and PackBits — the three most common compression types. If your TIFF uses Deflate, JPEG-in-TIFF, or CCITT compression, re-save it in one of the supported formats using a desktop editor first.

Can I batch convert TIFF to PNG?

Yes. Drop multiple TIFF files at once — imgready processes them in parallel and lets you download a ZIP file with all the PNG results.

Is it private?

Yes. imgready decodes TIFF entirely in your browser. Files never reach our servers. This is important for scanned documents, medical imaging, and confidential content.
