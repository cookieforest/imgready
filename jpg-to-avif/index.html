What makes AVIF better than JPG?

AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) is derived from the AV1 video codec developed by the Alliance for Open Media. It applies the same advanced compression techniques used in video — things like intra-prediction, transform coding, and loop filtering — to still images. The result is remarkable: an AVIF encoded at quality 60 often looks better than a JPG at quality 85, while being 40–50% smaller.

The practical difference is visible in two places. First, fine-grained textures (skin, fabric, foliage) look cleaner in AVIF because it handles high-frequency detail better than JPG's block-based DCT transform. Second, smooth gradients (sky, backgrounds) show less banding and fewer compression artefacts. For e-commerce product photos and hero images, the quality improvement at smaller file sizes is meaningful.

AVIF vs WebP: which should you use?

AVIF compresses more aggressively than WebP — files are typically 20–30% smaller than the equivalent WebP. The trade-off is encoding speed: AVIF takes longer to compress than WebP, which is why imgready uses WebAssembly encoders to keep it fast in the browser. For most web use cases, AVIF is the better choice if you care about maximum file size reduction. If you need faster processing or serve a significant audience on older browsers that support WebP but not AVIF, WebP remains a solid choice.

Pro tip: Use imgready's multi-format output to generate both AVIF and JPG in the same pass. Then serve the AVIF to modern browsers and fall back to the JPG for older ones using a <picture> element. You get maximum efficiency without breaking anything.

Quality settings for JPG to AVIF

AVIF quality numbers are not directly comparable to JPG quality numbers. AVIF quality 60 is roughly equivalent to JPG quality 85 in visual output. imgready defaults to quality 60 for AVIF, which produces excellent results for most photographic content. If you're converting screenshots or graphics with hard edges, try quality 70–75 to reduce the risk of edge artefacts.

AVIF vs WebP: which format to use?

Wondering whether to convert to AVIF or WebP? AVIF offers ~20–30% better compression than WebP, but WebP has slightly broader browser support (97% vs 93%). For a full comparison including encoding speed, HDR support, and practical recommendations, read the AVIF vs WebP guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is AVIF supported on iPhone?

Yes, since iOS 16 (September 2022). Safari on iOS 16 and later fully supports AVIF for both display and download.

Why does AVIF take longer to encode than JPG or WebP?

AVIF uses a more computationally intensive compression algorithm derived from video encoding. imgready uses Google's reference WebAssembly encoder, which is optimised for quality but not for speed. A batch of large images will take longer to encode as AVIF than as JPG.

Can I use AVIF on my WordPress site?

WordPress 6.5 and later supports AVIF uploads natively. For older versions, plugins like ShortPixel or Imagify can serve AVIF automatically.

Does AVIF support transparency?

Yes, AVIF has full alpha channel support. Unlike JPG, transparent areas are preserved correctly in AVIF output — making it a potential replacement for PNG in some web use cases.

How does AVIF compare to HEIC?

Both are derived from similar video compression technology, but AVIF is open and royalty-free while HEIC is patent-encumbered. AVIF has broad web browser support; HEIC is primarily used in Apple devices for camera storage. For web publishing, AVIF is the right choice.

Is AVIF supported in all browsers in 2026?

AVIF has approximately 93% global browser coverage as of 2026, supported by Chrome, Firefox, Safari 16+, and Edge. For maximum compatibility, serve AVIF as the primary format with a WebP or JPEG fallback using the HTML <picture> element. imgready exports pure AVIF — pair it with a WebP version from our JPG to WebP tool for a complete fallback stack.
