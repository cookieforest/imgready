When to pick PNG: If you need to edit the photo (remove a background, composite layers, adjust in Photoshop), always convert to PNG first. PNG's lossless encoding means no quality is lost before you start editing. For sharing or web publishing, JPG or WebP will be smaller.

Why HEIC to PNG is sometimes the right choice

HEIC and JPG are both lossy formats. Converting HEIC to JPG runs the image through a second round of lossy compression. For most casual use this is invisible. But if you need an archival copy — a photo that will be edited in Photoshop, printed at high resolution, used as the master for marketing assets, or stored as a long-term backup — running it through a second lossy round is a small but non-zero quality loss. PNG is lossless: the conversion preserves every pixel exactly as the HEIC decoder produced it.

The cost is file size. An HEIC photo around 3 MB typically expands to 8–15 MB as a PNG, depending on how complex the image content is. For a few photos that's fine; for a year's worth of iCloud Photos, the storage bill adds up. Use PNG only when lossless really matters; otherwise JPG or WebP is more practical.

What "lossless" actually buys you

  • Editing fidelity. Re-saving a JPG repeatedly degrades it. Re-saving a PNG never does. If your workflow involves multiple rounds of edits, PNG is the safe master.
  • Compositing and transparency. JPG cannot represent transparent pixels at all. PNG can. If you cut out an iPhone photo subject and want to drop it onto a different background, you need PNG (or WebP-lossless).
  • Print and archive. Lossless ensures the print pipeline never sees compression artefacts that might accumulate.

When PNG is the wrong answer

  • Sharing photos via email or messaging. The 4-5x larger file is wasteful and many email systems will choke. Use JPG.
  • Publishing photos on the web. A 12 MB PNG is hostile to mobile visitors. Use WebP (lossless or lossy depending on need).
  • Sending to a print shop that asks for JPG. Just send what they asked for.

How imgready does HEIC → PNG

imgready decodes the HEIC using the libheif-js WebAssembly library, producing raw RGBA pixel data. From there, the encode path depends on the quality slider:

  • Quality 100: the browser's native canvas PNG encoder writes a fully lossless PNG-24 — the typical answer for "convert HEIC to PNG".
  • Quality below 100: imgready quantizes the palette down to 16, 32, 64, 128, or 256 colors (depending on the slider) using UPNG.js, producing a much smaller PNG-8. This is rarely what you want for photographs (banding becomes visible at 256 colors), but is useful if your "HEIC" is a screenshot.

Do you actually need PNG, or is WebP enough?

For most "I want a lossless copy of this iPhone photo" use cases, WebP-lossless is a better choice. It's lossless, it preserves the alpha channel, and it's typically 26% smaller than the equivalent PNG. The only reason to insist on PNG is when the destination tool genuinely cannot read WebP — many older editing pipelines fall in that bucket.

Frequently asked questions

Is HEIC to PNG truly lossless?

Yes — at quality 100, imgready produces a fully lossless PNG. Every pixel from the decoded HEIC is preserved exactly. (At quality below 100, PNG-8 quantization kicks in and the result is technically lossy.)

Why is my PNG so much bigger than the HEIC?

PNG is lossless and HEIC is heavily compressed (lossy). A 3 MB HEIC commonly produces an 8–15 MB PNG. If you do not need lossless quality, JPG or WebP will be 70–90% smaller.

Does PNG preserve transparency from HEIC?

Yes. If the HEIC contains transparent regions (rare, but supported), imgready preserves the alpha channel through the conversion.

Should I use HEIC to PNG or HEIC to WebP-lossless?

WebP-lossless is typically 26% smaller than PNG with identical fidelity and alpha. Choose PNG only if your downstream tool cannot read WebP.

Are EXIF and GPS data preserved?

Not by default — imgready strips EXIF metadata for privacy. You can opt back in via Settings → Metadata if you need to keep camera and GPS data.

Can I convert HEIC to PNG in batch?

Yes. Drop multiple HEIC files into imgready and click Process — each one is converted to PNG and you can download them all in a ZIP.
