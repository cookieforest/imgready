Why you sometimes need PNG instead of SVG

SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics) is the ideal format for icons, logos, and illustrations on the web — it scales to any size without pixelation and produces tiny file sizes. But SVG isn't universally supported. Microsoft Office apps (Word, PowerPoint, Outlook) have inconsistent SVG rendering. Many email clients ignore SVG entirely and show a broken image. iOS and Android apps often require raster assets in their asset catalogs. PDF generators, slide tools, and print workflows frequently demand a PNG or JPG. In all of these cases, converting SVG to PNG is the practical solution.

Does SVG to PNG preserve transparency?

Yes. PNG natively supports full alpha transparency, so any transparent areas in your SVG come through correctly. If you're exporting a logo on a transparent background for placement over a dark background, PNG is the right format — unlike JPG, which would fill the transparent areas with white.

Tip: If your SVG has a white or coloured background rectangle baked into it (common in SVGs exported from Figma or Illustrator), you'll see that background in the PNG. To get a transparent-background PNG, open the SVG in a text editor and remove the background <rect> before dropping it into imgready.

SVG to PNG vs SVG to JPG

For most use cases, PNG is the better rasterisation target. JPG doesn't support transparency, so any transparent parts of your SVG become white in the output. The only reason to prefer JPG would be file size on a photographic SVG (rare) or a platform that doesn't accept PNG. For icons, logos, and anything with transparency, always export SVG to PNG.

Frequently asked questions

Will the PNG look sharp at large sizes?

Yes — SVG is a mathematical description of shapes, not pixels. imgready renders it at whatever pixel dimension you specify, so a 4000 px PNG from a 1 KB SVG will be perfectly crisp.

Can I convert an SVG with custom fonts?

It depends. If the SVG embeds the font glyphs as paths (common when exporting from Illustrator or Figma with 'Outline Text'), they convert perfectly. If the SVG references an external font by name and that font isn't available in the browser, the text will fall back to a system font. Convert text to paths before exporting if exact typography matters.

Is there a file size limit for SVG input?

No server-side limit because everything runs in your browser. Very complex SVGs with thousands of paths may take a moment to render, but there's no cap.

Can imgready convert multiple SVGs at once?

Yes. Drop a folder or select multiple SVGs and imgready processes them all. You can set the output width once and it applies to the whole batch.

Why does my SVG look different as a PNG than in the browser?

A few common reasons: external CSS or fonts not embedded in the SVG, filter effects that render differently between browsers, or a viewBox that clips part of the design. Check the SVG in a standalone browser tab first — what you see there is what imgready will rasterise.

What resolution should I use when converting SVG to PNG?

It depends on the use case. For standard web display, 1× (72–96 dpi) is fine. For retina / HiDPI screens, export at 2× your display size — a 400×400 SVG becomes a 800×800 PNG. For print, use 300 dpi minimum. imgready lets you set an exact pixel width before converting so you get the resolution you need.
