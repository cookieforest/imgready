What TIFF is and why you have one

TIFF (Tagged Image File Format) was created in 1986 and has been the gold-standard archival format for professional photography, print pre-press, scientific imaging, and document scanning ever since. Unlike JPG, TIFF can store images losslessly, with 16- or 32-bit color depth, full alpha channel, multiple layers, and a wide variety of metadata. Almost every professional camera supports TIFF as an output option, every flatbed scanner produces TIFF by default, and most print workflows expect TIFF or PSD as input.

The downside is the same as PNG times ten: TIFF files are enormous. A 24-megapixel TIFF can easily be 80–150 MB. Sharing one via email is impractical. Posting one on Instagram is impossible. Editing one in a non-professional tool can be tricky. Converting to JPG opens up everything.

TIFF compression types and what imgready supports

TIFF technically supports a dozen different internal compression schemes, but in practice three dominate: uncompressed (the simplest), LZW (lossless, common in print), and PackBits (lossless, common in older scanners and Mac-origin TIFFs). imgready handles all three natively. If your browser supports createImageBitmap() with TIFF (Chrome does in many cases), imgready uses that fast path; otherwise it falls back to a hand-written TIFF parser that handles the three common compression types.

TIFFs that use less common compression (Deflate, JPEG-in-TIFF, CCITT for fax, etc.) may not decode. If you have one, the easiest fix is to open it in any image editor and re-save as uncompressed or LZW TIFF, then run it through imgready.

Quality and resize for TIFF → JPG

Because TIFFs are usually high-resolution archival masters, you almost always want to resize during conversion. A 24-megapixel TIFF used as a hero image on a 1200-pixel-wide blog should be resized to 1600 pixels on the long side; the rest of the resolution is wasted bytes. Set the resize value in Settings (or pick a preset) before you process. Combined with quality 82, a 100 MB TIFF typically becomes a 200–300 KB JPG with no visible quality loss at the new size.

Privacy: where do TIFFs come from?

TIFFs are often scanned documents: tax returns, ID cards, contracts, medical records. Uploading those to a random online converter is a real privacy risk. imgready does the conversion entirely in your browser — your scanned passport never leaves your laptop. Same goes for medical-imaging TIFFs from MRI/CT scanners and forensic-grade photography exports.

When to convert TIFF to JPG vs other formats

JPG is the right choice when you need the file to open everywhere — email attachments, social media, client delivery, or printing at a lab. If you need to preserve transparency (TIFFs can have alpha channels), convert to PNG instead. If you're publishing to a website, WebP gives you 25–35% smaller files than JPG at the same quality. Frequently asked questions

What TIFF compressions does imgready support?

Uncompressed, LZW, and PackBits — the three most common. If your TIFF uses Deflate, JPEG-in-TIFF, or CCITT compression, you may need to re-save it in a more common format first using a desktop editor.

Why is my TIFF file so huge?

TIFFs are designed for archival quality, not file-size efficiency. A 24-megapixel uncompressed TIFF is around 100 MB. LZW compression brings that down maybe 30%. JPG, by contrast, compresses the same image to 1–4 MB at a quality indistinguishable from the source.

Will TIFF to JPG lose quality?

TIFFs are typically lossless and JPG is lossy, so technically yes. At imgready's default quality 82, the loss is invisible at typical viewing distances. For pixel-perfect output, convert to PNG instead.

Is TIFF to JPG private?

Yes. imgready decodes TIFF entirely in your browser. The file never reaches our servers. This matters for scanned documents, medical imaging, and other sensitive content.

Can imgready handle multi-page TIFFs?

imgready converts the first image plane in a TIFF. Multi-page TIFFs (often used for fax or scanned documents) only have their first page converted. For all pages, use a desktop tool like ImageMagick to split the TIFF first.

How can I convert TIFF to WebP instead of JPG?

Use the TIFF to WebP converter, or just toggle the format buttons after dropping a TIFF — imgready handles the same conversion to WebP, AVIF, or PNG.
