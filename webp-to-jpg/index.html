Why you might need JPG from a WebP file

WebP is an excellent format for web publishing — smaller files, better quality per byte, supported by every modern browser. But "supported everywhere on the web" is not the same as "supported everywhere, full stop." The situations where WebP causes problems include: sending images as email attachments (many corporate email clients show WebP as a broken attachment icon); uploading to platforms that require JPG or PNG specifically (certain e-commerce systems, print services, government portals, legacy CMSes); sharing with colleagues or clients who use older software; and opening on devices running older operating systems where WebP decoding isn't available.

In all of these cases, converting WebP to JPG is the practical solution. JPG has been universally supported since 1993 — every operating system, every email client, every printer driver, every image editing application handles it.

Will converting WebP to JPG lose quality?

WebP is a lossy format (by default), and JPG is also a lossy format. Converting from one lossy format to another always involves a second round of compression, which means a small additional quality loss is technically unavoidable. At imgready's default quality 82, this loss is invisible at normal viewing distance. If you need to minimise any quality degradation, set the JPG quality to 90 or above — you'll get slightly larger files but the difference from the original WebP will be undetectable.

For lossless WebP files (WebP can optionally be encoded losslessly), the conversion to JPG will apply lossy compression for the first time. Use quality 90+ for lossless-source WebP if the image will be printed or displayed at large sizes.

How imgready converts WebP to JPG

imgready decodes the WebP file using the browser's native WebP decoder (all modern browsers support this natively — no WebAssembly decoder required). The pixel data is then passed to imgready's JPG encoder, which applies the quality setting you've chosen and produces the output file. Because WebP decoding is native, this conversion is very fast — a batch of 50 WebP files takes only a few seconds on a modern device.

Converting WebP for email

JPEG is the right format for email attachments — WebP has poor support in Outlook and many email clients. After converting to JPG, check that your file size is under 1 MB per image for email. For more guidance on sizes and formats, see the compress images for email guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can imgready convert lossless WebP to JPG?

Yes. imgready decodes any valid WebP file — lossy, lossless, or animated (first frame) — and re-encodes it as JPG. For lossless WebP, use quality 90+ to minimise the quality loss on the first compression pass.

Does converting WebP to JPG affect file size?

It depends on the source quality. A WebP encoded at high quality that gets re-encoded to JPG at quality 82 typically produces a comparable or slightly larger file. WebP is more efficient than JPG at equal visual quality, so converting back will generally produce a bigger file for the same perceptual quality.

Can I convert animated WebP to JPG?

imgready extracts the first frame of an animated WebP and converts it to a static JPG. Full animated WebP to animated JPG conversion is not supported — JPG does not support animation.

My WebP was downloaded from a website — can I convert it?

Yes. imgready doesn't care where the file came from. Drop it in and convert it. Be aware of the original image's copyright status — converting a format doesn't grant any rights to use the image.

Why does my converted JPG look slightly different from the WebP?

Both WebP and JPG are lossy formats. Converting between them involves a second compression pass, which may introduce subtle differences — particularly in smooth gradients or areas with fine texture. At quality 82 the difference is invisible at typical screen viewing distances.

Why does my converted JPG look slightly different from the original?

Both WebP and JPG use lossy compression, so converting between them involves two compression passes — once when the WebP was created and once to JPG. This is normal and the quality difference is generally imperceptible at imgready's default quality 90. To minimize loss, use the highest quality setting available and avoid repeated round-trip conversions.
