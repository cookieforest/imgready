What is AVIF? The next-generation image format explained

AVIF is the newest image format, developed by the Alliance for Open Media and based on the AV1 video codec. It's a dramatic leap forward from JPEG — files are typically 50% smaller at the same visual quality, with no discernible loss in detail. AVIF supports transparency, HDR photography, animation, and wide color gamut. Here's what AVIF is, why it matters, which browsers support it, and when to use it for your images and websites.

Quick answer

AVIF stands for AV1 Image File Format. It's built on the AV1 video codec and produces images roughly 50% smaller than JPEG with superior quality. Browser support is excellent but not universal: about 93% of browsers can display AVIF as of 2026. For public websites, serve AVIF with WebP and JPEG fallbacks using the HTML picture element to ensure compatibility.

The real advantage: AVIF offers the best compression-to-quality ratio of any image format. For websites where bandwidth and page speed matter, AVIF can meaningfully reduce transfer sizes. The catch: encoding is slow, and you need fallback formats for older browsers.

Where AVIF came from

AVIF was developed by the Alliance for Open Media (AOM) starting around 2016, with the specification finalized in 2019. The AOM includes Google, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Intel, and dozens of other tech companies. The goal was to create a next-generation image format superior to both JPEG and WebP, while remaining royalty-free and open-source. AVIF is based on the AV1 video codec, which means it leverages decades of video compression research.

Why AV1 for images?

Video codecs like AV1 are far more sophisticated than image codecs because they need to compress 24–60 frames per second efficiently. By adapting AV1 for still images, AVIF inherited that sophistication. The result is compression that rivals proprietary, patent-heavy formats while remaining completely free and open.

AVIF compression: 50% smaller than JPEG

A typical photograph that's 200 KB as JPEG might be just 100 KB as AVIF. For a page with 10 hero images, that's 1 MB saved in transfer size. For a photography portfolio or e-commerce site with hundreds of product photos, the cumulative savings are enormous. This compression comes from AVIF's more sophisticated algorithms, not from reducing image quality.

Quality at low file sizes

AVIF's biggest advantage over WebP shows up when you're aggressively compressing images. A 50 KB AVIF file retains far more detail than a 50 KB WebP or JPEG. This is especially useful for thumbnails, mobile hero images, and bandwidth-constrained environments.

  <source srcset="image.webp" type="image/webp">
  <img src="image.jpg" alt="Your image">
</picture>

AVIF vs WebP: The encoding speed trade-off

AVIF encoding is significantly slower than WebP — typically 5 to 20 times slower depending on quality settings. A batch conversion that would take 5 minutes in WebP might take 30–60 minutes in AVIF. For pre-processed static websites, this one-time cost is acceptable. For real-time image generation (e.g., a CDN resizing uploaded photos on the fly), the encoding overhead can be problematic.

WebP is the pragmatic choice for systems that generate images on-demand. AVIF is the better choice for static assets processed once at build time.

Features AVIF supports

  • Lossy and lossless modes — Choose between maximum compression or absolute quality preservation
  • Transparency (alpha channel) — Like PNG, but with AVIF's superior compression
  • HDR photography — 10-bit and 12-bit color depth for high-dynamic-range images
  • Wide color gamut — Supports DCI-P3, Adobe RGB, and other professional color spaces
  • Animation — Like animated GIFs but more efficient
  • Multiple images per file — Can store image sequences in a single file
  • Depth maps — Stores 3D depth information for computational photography

When to use AVIF

AVIF is ideal for websites and applications where bandwidth and performance are critical. It's especially effective for photography-heavy sites, e-commerce product catalogs, and any project where maximum compression matters. AVIF makes sense when:

  • Your audience primarily uses modern browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari 16+, Edge)
  • You can implement a WebP/JPEG fallback stack
  • Images are processed once at build time, not dynamically generated
  • Photographic content dominates your site (not flat graphics or logos)
  • Bandwidth savings and page speed are top priorities

When to stick with WebP or JPEG

  • Your audience includes significant legacy browser traffic (IE, old Safari, Android)
  • You generate images in real-time and need fast encoding
  • Your site is mostly flat graphics, icons, and logos
  • You're migrating from JPEG and want simplicity without multiple fallbacks
  • Animated images dominate your content

Frequently asked questions

What is AVIF?

AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) is a modern image format developed by the Alliance for Open Media. It's based on the AV1 video codec and typically produces files 50% smaller than JPEG at equivalent quality. AVIF supports lossy and lossless compression, transparency, HDR photography, animation, and wide color gamut — making it the most feature-complete image format available in 2026.

Which browsers support AVIF?

As of 2026, AVIF is supported by Chrome 85+ (2020), Firefox 93+ (2021), Edge 121+ (2024), and Safari 16+ (2022). In total, approximately 93% of global browser traffic supports AVIF. The remaining gap consists mainly of older iOS Safari versions and legacy browsers. For maximum compatibility, serve AVIF with WebP or JPEG fallback using the HTML picture element.

Is AVIF better than WebP?

AVIF offers superior compression — typically 20–30% smaller files than WebP at the same quality. However, WebP has broader browser support (97% vs 93%) and much faster encoding speed. For photographic content and maximum compression, AVIF wins. For speed and universal compatibility, WebP is more practical. The best approach is using both with fallbacks.

Can Photoshop open AVIF?

Photoshop (2022 and later) supports AVIF opening and export. Older versions of Photoshop do not support AVIF natively. For desktop image editors, most modern software including Affinity Photo, GIMP, and Krita now support AVIF. For online viewing and conversion, use imgready or other browser-based tools.

How do I create an AVIF file?

The easiest approach for individuals is using a free online converter like imgready — just upload a JPG or PNG and download the AVIF. For developers, use command-line tools like ImageMagick, libavif, or online APIs. Most modern image processing libraries in JavaScript, Python, and Node.js now include AVIF support natively.

Is AVIF good for all images?

AVIF excels with photographs and high-detail images where its 50% compression advantage is most visible. For flat graphics, logos, and illustrations, AVIF's advantage narrows significantly — lossless PNG may remain the better choice. AVIF is not ideal for very small images where file size overhead barely matters. For most websites with photographic content, AVIF is the optimal choice.
