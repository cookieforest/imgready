Why convert AVIF back to JPG

AVIF is an excellent next-generation image format. Supported by modern web browsers, AVIF delivers extraordinary compression — often 20-50% smaller than JPG at identical visual quality. For web publishing and cloud storage, AVIF is brilliant. But "supported by modern browsers" doesn't mean "supported everywhere." AVIF adoption is still rolling out, and many real-world use cases require falling back to a format from the 1990s: JPG.

Situations where AVIF causes compatibility problems: sending images as email attachments (many corporate email clients don't display AVIF, showing a broken icon instead); uploading to legacy platforms that explicitly require JPG or PNG (older e-commerce sites, print services, government document portals); sharing with colleagues or clients using older software; opening on devices with older operating systems where AVIF decoding isn't available; and printing, where printer drivers often don't understand AVIF. In all of these cases, converting AVIF to JPG is the practical solution. JPG has been universally supported since 1993 — it opens everywhere.

When AVIF doesn't work

Microsoft Outlook is the big culprit. Many organizations run versions of Outlook (desktop or web) that predate AVIF support. When you attach an AVIF file to an email, recipients see a broken image icon instead of the photo. The same happens with Windows Photo Viewer on Windows 10 and older systems — it doesn't recognize AVIF. Some online print services explicitly reject AVIF uploads; older photo editing software doesn't open it; and legacy content management systems don't understand the format.

JPG, by contrast, has never failed. It's universally supported by every operating system, every email client, every web browser, every image editor, every printer driver, and every embedded device. JPG is the safe choice when you need a file to work everywhere.

Compatibility first: If you're unsure whether your recipient's email client or software supports AVIF, convert to JPG. It's always the safer bet. JPG is the lowest-common-denominator format — it will always work.

Quality considerations: AVIF to JPG

AVIF is a lossy format (by default), and JPG is also lossy. Converting between them involves a second compression pass, which technically causes some additional quality loss. However, this loss is imperceptible at imgready's default JPG quality setting of 82. At 82, the difference between the original AVIF and the converted JPG is invisible at typical screen viewing distances, and certainly undetectable in email or web contexts.

If your AVIF was encoded at very high quality or contains fine detail (text, diagrams, high-frequency patterns), use JPG quality 90 or above. This produces a larger output file but guarantees that the conversion artifact is completely invisible. Avoid quality below 75 unless your image is photographic and file size is critical — lower settings introduce visible compression blocks, particularly in areas with smooth gradients.

Frequently asked questions

Why would I need to convert AVIF to JPG?

AVIF is a next-generation image format with exceptional compression, but it's not yet universally supported. Older software like Windows Photo Viewer, many email clients (especially Outlook), some printers, and legacy content management systems don't recognize AVIF files. Converting to JPG ensures compatibility with any application built in the last 30 years.

Will converting AVIF to JPG lose quality?

AVIF is typically a lossy format, and JPG is also lossy. Converting between them involves a second compression pass, causing minor quality loss. However, at imgready's default quality 82, this loss is imperceptible at normal viewing distances. For critical images, use quality 90+ to minimize degradation.

Which software can't open AVIF files?

AVIF support is still rolling out. Applications without native AVIF support include: Windows Photo Viewer (pre-Windows 11), older versions of Adobe Creative Suite, Outlook and many email clients, most photo editing software published before 2023, many online printing services, and some embedded systems. JPG, by contrast, opens in everything.

Is my data private when I convert AVIF to JPG?

Yes. All conversion happens in your browser, locally on your device. Your files never touch imgready's servers. The source AVIF and output JPG never leave your computer. This is true even during batch conversion — everything runs in-browser.

What's the difference in file size between AVIF and JPG?

AVIF typically achieves 20-50% better compression than JPG at the same visual quality. When you convert AVIF to JPG, you're trading that efficiency for universal compatibility — the JPG output will be noticeably larger than the source AVIF, but smaller than the original uncompressed image.
