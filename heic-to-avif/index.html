HEIC, AVIF, and why this conversion matters

iPhones have shot in HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) by default since iOS 11. HEIC is Apple's format based on the HEVC codec — it stores photos at roughly half the size of JPEG with no visible quality loss, which is why Apple uses it. The problem is compatibility: HEIC isn't natively supported by most web browsers, Windows, Android, or web upload forms.

AVIF is the natural next step. It's an open, modern format based on the AV1 codec that achieves even better compression than HEIC — and, critically, it's supported by all major browsers in 2026 (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari 16+). Converting your iPhone photos from HEIC to AVIF means smaller files that work everywhere on the modern web.

When HEIC to AVIF is the right choice

HEIC to AVIF is ideal when you're uploading iPhone photos to a website or web app where you control the HTML and can use a <picture> element with a JPEG fallback. It's the best-compression option for product photography, portfolio images, blog post photos, and any website image where file size and quality both matter.

Need maximum compatibility instead? If you're sharing with Windows users, attaching to email, or uploading to a platform that doesn't support AVIF, use HEIC to JPG or HEIC to PNG instead. JPEG works everywhere; AVIF works on 93% of modern browsers.

Recommended quality settings

Since HEIC is already compressed, converting to AVIF at a higher quality setting (85–90) minimizes the double-compression penalty while still delivering meaningful file size savings over keeping the original HEIC. For web thumbnails and previews, quality 75–80 is fine. For full-size portfolio or product photos, quality 85 is the recommended default.

HEIC to AVIF vs HEIC to WebP

AVIF produces files roughly 20–30% smaller than WebP at equivalent quality. WebP has slightly better browser support (97% vs 93%). For maximum compression on modern websites, choose AVIF. For broader compatibility with a single format, choose WebP. Use imgready's HEIC to WebP converter if WebP is a better fit for your workflow.

Frequently asked questions

What is HEIC and why do iPhones use it?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is Apple's default photo format, introduced with iOS 11. It uses the HEVC codec and stores photos at roughly half the size of JPEG with the same visual quality. The tradeoff is compatibility — HEIC is not natively supported on Windows, Android, or most web platforms without conversion.

Why convert HEIC to AVIF instead of JPEG?

AVIF is a modern, universally efficient format that compresses about 50% better than JPEG and 20–30% better than WebP. If you're optimizing iPhone photos for a website, AVIF is the best target format in 2026 — better quality at smaller file sizes than JPEG, with 93% browser support. For email or apps requiring maximum compatibility, HEIC to JPG is the safer choice.

Does HEIC to AVIF conversion lose quality?

HEIC is already a compressed format, so converting to AVIF applies a second round of compression. At imgready's default quality 85, the visual difference is imperceptible at normal viewing sizes. For archival purposes, use lossless AVIF mode — though lossless AVIF files will be larger than the original HEIC.

Is AVIF supported on all browsers in 2026?

AVIF has approximately 93% global browser support in 2026, covering Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari 16+. For maximum compatibility when publishing on the web, serve AVIF as the primary format with a JPEG fallback using the HTML <picture> element.
