Warning: JPG to PNG won't restore lost quality. If your JPG was heavily compressed, converting it to PNG preserves those compression artefacts losslessly — it doesn't remove them. PNG is the right format if you need to edit further or keep transparency; it won't make a blurry JPG sharp again.

Should you convert JPG to PNG?

This is the conversion direction that needs the most thought, because PNG is almost always larger than JPG and lossless conversion of an already-lossy file does not magically restore quality. The reason to do it anyway is that PNG is your master format from this point forward — every future edit, save, and re-export will be lossless. JPG is destructive: every save degrades the image a tiny bit.

So the right question is: "What am I going to do with this image next?" If the answer is "edit it in Photoshop, then save it again, then maybe edit it again", convert to PNG first to break the JPG→JPG degradation chain. If the answer is "post it on a blog and never touch it again", stay with JPG (or convert to WebP for size).

When JPG to PNG actually makes sense

  • Editing pipelines. Anything you'll re-save multiple times. PNG is lossless; JPG isn't.
  • Adding transparency. JPG cannot represent transparent pixels. To cut out a subject from a JPG and save with transparency, you must save the cut-out as PNG.
  • Graphic design imports. Many design tools and stock-asset pipelines expect PNG.
  • Repeated re-export. Anything saved over and over again should be PNG to avoid generation loss.

When JPG to PNG is the wrong answer

  • You think it'll improve quality. It won't. The JPG already lost data; PNG just preserves what's left.
  • You want a smaller file. PNG is almost always 4–8x bigger than the JPG. For smaller files, convert to WebP instead.
  • You want to publish on the web. PNG is much heavier than JPG or WebP for photographs. Use WebP-lossy or stay with JPG.

PNG-24 vs PNG-8: which does imgready output?

imgready picks based on the quality slider. Quality 100 produces PNG-24 — full 16.7-million-color, fully lossless, the format Photoshop expects when you "Save As PNG". Quality below 100 triggers UPNG.js color quantization, producing PNG-8 with a palette of 256 colors or fewer. PNG-8 files are typically 40–70% smaller than PNG-24, but with visible color banding on photographs. The default for JPG → PNG conversions is quality 100 because the use case is usually "I want lossless".

Frequently asked questions

Will JPG to PNG improve image quality?

No. PNG is lossless, but the JPG source is already lossy — the data that JPG discarded cannot be recovered. PNG preserves what's there now and prevents further loss on subsequent edits.

Why is the PNG file so much bigger than the JPG?

PNG is lossless, JPG is heavily compressed (lossy). For a typical photograph, the PNG can be 4–8x larger than the JPG. If you don't need lossless, stay with JPG or use WebP.

Can I add transparency to a JPG by converting to PNG?

The conversion alone won't add transparency — your JPG has no alpha channel. But once converted to PNG, you can edit out the background in Photoshop (or any image editor) and save with transparency intact.

Should I use PNG-24 or PNG-8?

imgready uses PNG-24 (full color, lossless) at quality 100, and PNG-8 (palette quantized) at quality below 100. For photographs, stick with quality 100. For UI screenshots and flat illustrations, dropping to 85–95 produces much smaller files with imperceptible quality loss.
