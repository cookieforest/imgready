What AVIF is and why it compresses so well

AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) is the still-picture sibling of the AV1 video codec. AV1 was developed by the Alliance for Open Media — Google, Netflix, Amazon, Mozilla, and many others — to replace HEVC and VP9 as the next-generation video codec. Because AV1 is genuinely a generation ahead of older codecs, AVIF inherits compression efficiency that older image formats cannot match. On photographic content, AVIF at quality 50 is typically indistinguishable from JPG at quality 90, with about half the file size.

AVIF supports everything modern web design needs: full 8-, 10-, and 12-bit color depth, alpha-channel transparency, wide-gamut color (BT.2020, Display-P3), HDR, and even animation. Browser support reached majority share in late 2023 — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, Opera, and Samsung Internet all decode AVIF natively today.

When AVIF is the right choice

  • Photographic hero images on a fast-loading site, where every kilobyte counts toward Core Web Vitals.
  • E-commerce product galleries with many large photos and you control the audience's browser support.
  • Modern web apps with a known-modern user base where you can drop legacy browser support entirely.
  • Photography portfolios where the photographer wants pristine quality and is happy to serve a JPG fallback.

When NOT to use AVIF (yet)

  • Encoder time matters. AVIF encodes 5–10x slower than JPG or WebP. For real-time encoding (e.g. user-generated content uploads), WebP is still the better default.
  • You need universal compatibility with very old devices, kiosks, embedded systems, or older email clients — JPG remains safer there.
  • The image is mostly flat color or text. For UI screenshots and illustrations, WebP-lossless or PNG-8 often produces equally small files in a fraction of the time.

AVIF vs WebP — which should you use?

AVIF and WebP both beat JPEG on compression, but they work differently and suit different use cases. For a detailed breakdown of compression ratios, browser support, and encoding speed, see the AVIF vs WebP comparison guide. Short version: AVIF compresses 20–30% better than WebP, but WebP has slightly broader browser support. For maximum performance, serve AVIF with a WebP fallback.

Frequently asked questions

How much smaller is AVIF vs JPG?

On photographic content, AVIF is typically 40–50% smaller than JPG at the same visible quality. Savings can reach 60% on certain images.

How is AVIF different from WebP?

Both are modern image formats designed to replace JPG. WebP is older (2010) and broader in support; AVIF (2019) compresses tighter at the cost of slower encoding and slightly less universal browser support.

Why does AVIF encoding take so long?

AVIF is based on the AV1 video codec, which uses much more sophisticated compression than JPG or WebP. The result is a smaller file but the encoder has to do more work — typically 5–10x the compute. imgready runs encoding in your browser, so it'll be slower on phones and older laptops.

Does AVIF support transparency?

Yes — AVIF has a full alpha channel, just like PNG and WebP. AVIF-lossless preserves every pixel exactly with full transparency.

Should I serve AVIF directly or use a fallback?

Use a fallback. Browser support for AVIF is around 95% globally as of 2026, but for production sites it's still best to use the element with AVIF, WebP, and JPG sources so every visitor gets the best format their browser can handle.

Can imgready convert HEIC to AVIF?

Yes. imgready decodes HEIC/HEIF in-browser, then re-encodes as AVIF. The savings are significant — a typical 3 MB HEIC becomes a 200–400 KB AVIF.
