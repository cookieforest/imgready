Why PNG files are so large — and how AVIF fixes it

PNG uses lossless compression, which means it stores every pixel exactly. That's the right choice for screenshots, graphics, and images with hard edges or transparency. But for photographic content — a product photo, a hero image, a background — lossless compression is overkill. You're storing millions of pixels with full precision when the human eye can't tell the difference between the exact pixel value and one that's very close. AVIF applies lossy compression that exploits this perceptual limit, reducing file sizes by 40–70% compared to PNG with no visible quality loss at typical viewing sizes.

When to convert PNG to AVIF (and when not to)

AVIF works best on photographic PNG files — images with many colours, gradual gradients, and natural textures. It is less suited to:

  • Screenshots with text. Lossy compression can blur text edges. Use lossless WebP or keep PNG for screenshots.
  • Icons and logos. SVG is better for these. If you need raster, PNG or lossless WebP preserves hard edges cleanly.
  • Images that will be re-edited. AVIF is a delivery format, not a working format. Keep the PNG master and export AVIF for publication.

For everything else — product photography, hero images, blog illustrations, portfolio pieces — PNG to AVIF is one of the best optimisations you can make.

How imgready converts PNG to AVIF

imgready uses the @jsquash/avif WebAssembly encoder — Google's reference AV1 encoder compiled to run in the browser. It decodes your PNG to raw pixel data, runs the AVIF encoding pass entirely in memory, and hands back the finished AVIF file. None of this requires a network request. The libavif encoder is approximately 2.5 MB and is downloaded once on your first AVIF conversion, then cached for subsequent files.

AVIF vs WebP for PNG originals

PNG is lossless — converting it to either AVIF or WebP applies compression for the first time. Both formats handle PNG originals well. AVIF produces smaller files, while WebP is faster to encode and has slightly higher browser support. See the AVIF vs WebP comparison to decide which is right for your use case.

Frequently asked questions

Will my PNG's transparency survive the AVIF conversion?

Yes. AVIF natively supports alpha transparency. Transparent areas in your PNG will appear correctly in the AVIF output. This is one advantage AVIF has over JPG.

Is PNG to AVIF lossless or lossy?

By default, AVIF encoding is lossy — imgready uses quality 60, which produces excellent visual results at a fraction of the PNG file size. If you need lossless output (pixel-perfect preservation), keep the PNG or use lossless WebP instead.

How do I use AVIF on my website alongside older browser support?

Use the HTML element: place first, then as the fallback. Browsers that support AVIF will use it; others fall back to PNG automatically.

My PNG is a screenshot — should I convert it to AVIF?

Probably not. AVIF is optimised for photographic content. Screenshots with text and sharp UI elements may show slight edge blurring at high compression. Keep screenshots as PNG or lossless WebP for best results.

Can I batch-convert a folder of PNGs to AVIF?

Yes. Drop the entire folder into imgready and click Process All. All PNGs (and any other supported formats) will be encoded to AVIF in sequence. Download all results as a ZIP in one click.

Is AVIF better than PNG for website images?

For photographs and complex graphics, AVIF wins on file size — typically 50–70% smaller than PNG with near-identical visual quality. PNG remains the right choice when pixel-perfect lossless fidelity is required (logos, icons, screenshots with text). For hero images, product photos, and banners on your website, AVIF is the better pick in 2026.
