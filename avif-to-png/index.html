Why convert AVIF to PNG

AVIF is excellent for web publishing — smaller files, better compression than nearly every other format. But when you need to edit an image, preserve transparency, or maintain quality through multiple save cycles, PNG is often the better choice. PNG is a lossless format, meaning no quality is lost during compression. More importantly, PNG is still more widely supported than AVIF in design tools, print workflows, and legacy software. Converting AVIF to PNG is especially useful when you're building a design, need transparent backgrounds for layering, or plan to export the image multiple times without quality degradation.

Additionally, AVIF's transparency support (the alpha channel) is excellent but not universally compatible with older image editing software. PNG transparency, on the other hand, has been fully supported for 25+ years across every image editor, web browser, and design tool. If you need to guarantee transparency will work everywhere, PNG is the safe choice.

When to use PNG output over JPG

Choose PNG if you need transparency (logos with transparent backgrounds, product images on white backgrounds that you'll later composite onto other backgrounds, icons, illustrations with cutout edges). Choose PNG if you plan to edit the image in Photoshop, Figma, or other design software — lossless storage means the image won't degrade further when you re-save it. Choose PNG if the image contains text or sharp edges (gradients or soft shadows are fine in JPG; text and crisp lines degrade noticeably in lossy compression). Choose JPG only if you're publishing directly to the web and need the smallest possible file size, and you don't have transparency or fine text details.

For designers: PNG is the standard intermediate format for design workflows. Convert your AVIF source to PNG for editing, then export to JPG or AVIF once the design is final. This workflow avoids generational quality loss.

Transparency and alpha channels in AVIF and PNG

Both AVIF and PNG support full alpha channels (transparency). When you convert AVIF to PNG, if the original AVIF has transparency, the PNG output will preserve it perfectly. This is one of the key reasons to choose PNG — if you're working with transparent images, converting to PNG guarantees that transparency will be recognized by any software you use afterward. AVIF transparency is relatively new and still being rolled out across design software; PNG transparency is universal and has been standard since 1996.

Frequently asked questions

Why would I convert AVIF to PNG?

PNG is a lossless format ideal for image editing, design workflows, and preserving transparency. If your AVIF file has an alpha channel (transparency) or you plan to edit the image further, convert to PNG to avoid quality loss. PNG is also more widely supported than AVIF in legacy software and print workflows.

Does AVIF support transparency?

Yes. AVIF supports an alpha channel (transparency) just like PNG. However, when you convert AVIF with transparency to JPG (which has no transparency support), the transparent areas become opaque. When converting to PNG, transparency is fully preserved.

What's the difference in file size between AVIF and PNG?

AVIF typically achieves better compression than PNG, especially for photographic images. However, PNG is lossless while AVIF is usually lossy. When you convert AVIF to PNG, you're prioritizing quality preservation and editability over file size. The PNG output will be significantly larger than the source AVIF.

Is the conversion private and secure?

Yes. All conversion happens entirely in your browser. Your AVIF files and the output PNG files never leave your device or touch imgready's servers. Batch conversion, file size, and content are all processed locally on your device.

Can I batch convert multiple AVIF files to PNG?

Yes. Drop dozens of AVIF files at once and imgready converts them all to PNG simultaneously. There's no limit on batch size or file count. All processing happens in your browser at full speed.

When should I use PNG instead of JPG for the output?

Use PNG when you need transparency, plan to edit the image further, or need lossless quality preservation. Use JPG when you need the smallest possible file size and don't need transparency. PNG is better for graphics, logos, and designs; JPG is better for photographs and web images.
